# 5 Whys

**Starting point:** cold outbound to UK and Irish SMBs gets few replies, and the replies it does get rarely turn into meetings.

1. **Why are replies low?**
   Most emails reach people who have no reason to care this month. A 60-person company with everyone in Manchester doesn't need an employer of record.

2. **Why do the emails reach the wrong people?**
   Lists are built on firmographics (size, sector, location) instead of a trigger. Firmographics say who *could* buy. They don't say who has a decision to make *now*.

3. **Why are lists built that way?**
   Firmographic filters are quick to pull from a database. Spotting triggers takes judgement: reading job ads, team pages and announcements, and noticing that the role is in Poland and not London.

4. **Why isn't that judgement already part of the routine?**
   There's no repeatable method for it. Without a scoring rule and a weekly slot, research gets squeezed out by activity targets and the SDR falls back on volume.

5. **Why does that matter so much in SMB specifically?**
   SMB deals are small and fast, so the SDR's hours are the scarcest resource. An hour spent on a company with no live trigger is an hour not spent on one whose offer letter goes out next week.

**Root cause:** there's no repeatable, weekly system that turns public cross-border triggers into a ranked list with copy ready to send.

**What this repo does about it:**
- The board makes triggers the entry ticket. No signal, no sequence.
- The score turns judgement into five rules anyone can audit.
- The sequences are written around each trigger, so every email is about the prospect's own situation.
- The weekly rhythm protects Monday for research and Friday for learning.
