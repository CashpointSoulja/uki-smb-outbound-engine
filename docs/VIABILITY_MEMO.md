# Viability memo: why this approach fits the SMB SDR seat for UK & Ireland

**From:** Ayo Ahmed
**Re:** Sales Development Representative (SMB, UKI), Deel
**Bottom line:** the seat rewards whoever finds the companies with a live cross-border decision first and talks to them like a person. This repo shows the system I'd run to do that from week one, and how I'd prove or kill each part of it with real data.

---

## 1. The opportunity in the seat

- **The market is wide and shallow.** UK and Irish SMBs (10–200 staff) are numerous. Any one of them needs Deel only around a specific event: a first hire abroad, a growing contractor pool, or a new market. Timing beats volume.
- **The triggers are public.** Job ads say where a role is based. Team pages and credits show contractor footprints. Expansion news is announced on purpose. An SDR who reads these well can build a better list than any static filter.
- **The UK and Ireland lean outward.** Teams here routinely hire into the EU after Brexit, build nearshore engineering teams, and expand into the US and Europe early. All three segments recur every week.

## 2. Why this approach is viable

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can one SDR run it every week? | Yes. The rhythm budgets Monday for research (about 40 accounts), Tuesday to Thursday for touches and calls, and Friday for review. Nothing needs tools beyond a CRM, a sequencer, LinkedIn and job boards. |
| Does it need engineering support? | No. v1 is a static page. A live version would live inside the CRM; the board and score are just fields and a formula. |
| Is the messaging safe for the brand? | It makes no product claims and no statistics. It asks about the prospect's situation and offers practical help (checklists, sequencing). It's plain, British and short. |
| Is it measurable? | Every stage is defined in [METRICS.md](METRICS.md), and the score has a built-in validation test (tier A should beat tier C). |
| What if it doesn't work? | The weekly review is designed to find out fast. Each Friday changes one variable: a subject line, a segment or a score weight. |

## 3. What I'd do in the first 90 days

**Days 1–30: learn and copy.** Shadow AEs' discovery calls to learn which triggers actually turn into deals. Rebuild the board on real accounts inside Deel's CRM. Run the sequences on tier A only, adapted to the team's existing playbooks and compliance rules. Put every reply into the objection log.

**Days 31–60: run the rhythm.** Hit the weekly inputs consistently. First score check: is tier A outperforming tier C on contact-to-meeting? Rewrite the weakest touch in each sequence based on what people actually replied.

**Days 61–90: compound.** Share what's working with the SDR team: the best-performing trigger, subject lines and the handoff brief. Propose one change to how the team sources signals, with the data behind it.

## 4. Risks and honest limits

- **Every number in this repo is synthetic.** I haven't run these sequences against real Deel prospects, and I'm not claiming any reply or meeting rates. The first 30 days are for getting real baselines.
- **The score weights are my starting hypothesis.** They will be wrong in places, and that's what the validation step is for.
- **Deel's existing process comes first.** If the team already has a trigger list or sequences that work, I'd plug into those and use this as a way of thinking, not a replacement.
- **Consent and compliance.** A live version must follow UK GDPR/PECR for B2B outreach, honour opt-outs immediately, and keep to Deel's own policies.

## 5. Why me

I built this in my own time as an independent concept. It covers the list, the score, the copy, the dashboard, the operating rhythm, the documentation, the tests and the deployment. Building the machine is how I learned where it breaks. I'd bring that habit to the seat: work the territory hard, measure it honestly, and improve the system every week.
